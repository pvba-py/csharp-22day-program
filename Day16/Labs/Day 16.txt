**ClinicalBERT** is a specialized version of the **BERT (Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers)** language model that is designed for understanding and processing clinical and healthcare-related text. While the original BERT model is pre-trained on general-domain data such as books and Wikipedia articles, ClinicalBERT is further trained on large collections of clinical notes and electronic health records (EHRs), enabling it to better understand medical terminology, abbreviations, and the context used in healthcare documentation. ClinicalBERT leverages BERT's bidirectional transformer architecture, which allows it to capture relationships between words by considering both left and right contexts simultaneously. This makes it highly effective for clinical natural language processing (NLP) tasks such as disease classification, medical concept extraction, clinical outcome prediction, patient risk assessment, and information retrieval from medical records. By incorporating domain-specific knowledge from clinical text, ClinicalBERT achieves higher accuracy than general-purpose language models on healthcare-related tasks and has become an important tool for medical AI applications, clinical decision support systems, and healthcare data analytics.

For reference, ClinicalBERT was introduced by Emily Alsentzer and colleagues, who demonstrated that further pre-training BERT on clinical notes significantly improves performance on clinical NLP benchmarks. The model has since been widely adopted in medical research and healthcare-focused machine learning projects.
